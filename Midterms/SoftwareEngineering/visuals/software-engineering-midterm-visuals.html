Part A · Short questions

Fall 2023 · Question 1 · 2 marks

What is the Spiral Model in software development and why is it special?

Study answer. Spiral is an iterative, risk-driven process in which the product evolves through repeated cycles of planning, risk assessment, engineering and evaluation. Its distinguishing feature is explicit risk management combined with stakeholder feedback before committing to further development. It suits complex/high-risk work but needs experienced people and sound risk analysis.

Exam line

Spiral combines repeated cycles, explicit risk assessment and stakeholder feedback.

Source: W1 Ch02 s9,18; W3 Ch04 s6-7. Notes: Process models.

Fall 2023 · Question 2 · 2 marks

Why do Agile teams need customer collaboration?

Study answer. Collaboration clarifies goals and priorities, supplies frequent feedback on working increments, and lets the team adjust when needs change. It reduces the risk of completing a technically correct product that solves the wrong problem.

Exam line

Customer collaboration clarifies priorities, validates working software and helps teams adapt to change.

Source: W3 Ch03 s2,4-6; Ch04 s8-9. Notes: Agility.

Fall 2023 · Question 3 · 2 marks

Why do we need requirement analysis in software development?

Study answer. Requirements analysis refines stakeholder needs into understandable information, functional and behavioral models, identifies interfaces and constraints, and reveals ambiguity, omissions and conflicts before design and coding. It provides a basis for design, testing and stakeholder agreement.

Exam line

Requirements analysis clarifies information, functions, behavior, interfaces and constraints before design.

Source: W4 Ch07 s2-3,16-17; W5 Ch08 s2-6. Notes: Purpose and models.

Fall 2023 · Question 4 · 2 marks

List any four reasons why software projects can run into problems.

Study answer. Four valid examples are unrealistic deadlines, underestimated effort/resources, changing requirements without revising the plan, and poor communication. Other slide-supported examples include unconsidered risks, technical difficulties and failure to detect/correct slippage.

Exam line

Unrealistic deadlines, underestimated effort, unmanaged requirement change and poor communication can derail projects.

Source: W2 Ch24 s6,8-9; Ch25 s22. Notes: Late projects.

Fall 2023 · Question 5 · 2 marks

Provide a brief explanation of data modeling.

Study answer. Data modeling describes the information domain: relevant objects, their attributes and relationships, independently of the processing logic. For example, Employee has an identifier and department, while LeaveRequest relates an employee to dates and a request status.

Exam line

Data modeling describes domain objects, their attributes and relationships independently of processing logic.

Source: W5 Ch08 s3; W6 Ch09 s19. Notes: Data design.

Fall 2023 · Question 6 · 2 marks

Explain requirement modeling for WebApps in simple terms.

Study answer. It turns stakeholder goals and scenarios into models of what a WebApp must provide: content, user interaction, processing functions, navigation and the operating configuration. These models reveal missing or inconsistent requirements before implementation. For a booking app, this includes flight information, the booking interaction, fare processing and movement from search to confirmation.

Supplement boundary: Pressman/Maxim 8e Ch11 §11.5, printed pp214–218; this is beyond detailed coverage in the supplied 9e lecture decks.

Exam line

WebApp analysis models content, interaction, function, navigation and configuration.

Source: supplied Pressman/Maxim 8e, Ch11 section 11.5, printed pp214-218. This is textbook context beyond detailed coverage in the supplied 9e lecture decks. Notes: WebApp supplement.

Fall 2024 · Part A · Question 1 · 2 marks

What is a Legacy System? Why should it be replaced?

A legacy system is an existing, often long-lived system that still supports business operations. Modernization may be required to meet new business needs, use new technology, integrate with newer systems or operate in a network environment.

Replacement is justified when the required change cannot be achieved safely or economically through adaptation. Age alone does not establish that replacement is necessary.

Exam line

Legacy systems still support the business. New environments, business requirements, interoperability and network needs can justify change; assess adaptation and replacement against the actual gap.

Source: W1 Ch01 s6. · Notes: Legacy software › 3. Legacy software and reasons for change

Fall 2024 · Part A · Question 2 · 2 marks

Explain the Waterfall Software Development Process. When should it be used, and when should it not be used?

Waterfall organizes communication → planning → modeling → construction → deployment primarily in sequence. Construction includes coding and testing; deployment includes delivery, support and feedback.

It is reasonable when requirements are well understood, stable and predictable. It fits poorly when needs are uncertain or change frequently, because late clarification can require revisiting earlier work and redoing dependent work.

Exam line

Waterfall follows communication, planning, modeling, construction and deployment. Stable requirements suit its sequential structure; frequent change exposes the cost of late feedback and rework.

Source: W1 Ch02 s7,16. · Notes: Waterfall › 4. Waterfall

Fall 2024 · Part A · Question 3 · 2 marks

What is Content Analysis in requirement modeling for WebApps? Explain it.

Content analysis determines what information a WebApp must provide, the content objects involved, their attributes and their relationships. Read user scenarios to find direct and indirect references to information, then organize those objects in a list, model or data tree.

The resulting content model exposes missing or inconsistent information before presentation and navigation design. Flight details, fare information and booking-confirmation content are illustrative airline examples.

This answer uses the supplied Pressman/Maxim 8e textbook. Its chapter number does not identify a chapter in the 9e lecture decks.

Exam line

Content analysis identifies the information users need, its content objects and attributes, and their relationships from scenarios; it produces a content model that exposes omissions before design.

Source: supplied Pressman/Maxim 8e Ch11 section 11.5.4, printed pp216-217; textbook context beyond detailed 9e slide coverage. · Notes: Content analysis › Supplement §3. Content analysis and content model

Fall 2024 · Part A · Question 4 · 2 marks

What factors should a Software Project Manager consider when choosing a Software Project Team?

Consider seven factors when choosing the team structure:

  • Problem difficulty and product size.
  • Team lifetime and the possible degree of modularization.
  • Required quality and reliability.
  • Rigidity of the delivery date.
  • Communication and sociability required.

Match skills and experience to the work, and support compatible collaboration. These factors determine organization and coordination as well as staffing numbers.

Exam line

Team structure depends on difficulty, size, lifetime, modularity, quality/reliability, deadline rigidity and communication. Match skills and coordination to those constraints.

Source: W2 Ch24 s5-9. · Notes: Team factors › 4. Choosing a team structure

Fall 2024 · Part A · Question 6 · 2 marks

Briefly explain the CRC (Class-Responsibility-Collaborator) Model.

A CRC card identifies an analysis class, its responsibilities and the classes it collaborates with. Put the class name at the top, responsibilities on the left and collaborators on the right.

Walk through a use case and ask which card handles each interaction. A missing responsibility or collaboration leads to revised or new cards. For example, Booking can record itinerary and status while collaborating with Customer, Flight and Payment.

Exam line

CRC means Class, Responsibility and Collaborator. Review the cards by walking through a use case and checking who fulfills each responsibility and which other classes are needed.

Source: W5 Ch08 s20-22. · Notes: CRC › 5. CRC modeling

Draw a class rectangle with compartments for name, attributes and operations. A solid line is an association; multiplicities such as 1 and 0..* describe permitted related instances.

A generalization uses a hollow triangle pointing toward the parent class. Aggregation uses a hollow diamond at the whole end.

Part A · Short questions

Fall 2025 · Question 1 · 3 marks

How does software engineering help make software reliable and error-free?

What to write

  • SE uses systematic requirements analysis, structured design, reviews, testing and controlled changes to prevent, detect and correct defects.
  • Traceability helps verify that the delivered functions meet requirements, while modularity and regression checks reduce unintended effects.
  • These practices improve reliability; they do not prove absolute freedom from errors, because exhaustive testing is generally impossible.
Exam line

Disciplined requirements, design, reviews, testing and change control improve reliability. Exhaustive testing is generally impossible.

Source: W1 Ch01 s7-17; W4 Ch06 s16-21 · Notes: Testing principles

Fall 2025 · Question 2 · 3 marks

Why is the Waterfall model not good for changing requirements?

What to write

  • Its primarily sequential structure makes later work depend on earlier requirements/design decisions.
  • A late requirement change can force earlier artifacts and already completed code/tests to be revised.
  • Customer approval/testing late in the process can delay the discovery of these changes.
Exam line

Sequential dependencies and late feedback can make a requirement change force revisions to earlier artifacts and completed code and tests.

Source: W1 Ch02 s7,16 · Notes: Waterfall

Fall 2025 · Question 3 · 3 marks

How does Agile save time when customer needs change often?

What to write

  • Short working increments and frequent collaboration reveal changes early.
  • The team reprioritizes upcoming work and validates the next increment instead of implementing a large obsolete plan.
  • This reduces avoidable rework; it does not guarantee that every change is cheap or that every agile project is faster.
Exam line

Short increments, frequent collaboration and reprioritization reveal change early and reduce avoidable rework; savings depend on the project.

Source: W3 Ch03 s2-6; Ch04 s8-9 · Notes: Agile process

Fall 2025 · Question 4 · 3 marks

Why is it better to build software step-by-step?

What to write

  • Smaller increments are easier to understand, implement and evaluate.
  • Early releases deliver useful capabilities and feedback, expose risks before the full investment is committed, and permit scope/estimates to be revised.
  • Integration and regression testing are still needed to keep increments consistent.
Exam line

Build and evaluate small working increments to obtain early value, feedback and risk evidence. Keep integration and regression checks.

Source: W3 Ch04 s4,16-23 · Notes: Recommended process

Fall 2025 · Question 5 · 3 marks

How can a team leader keep developers motivated?

What to write

  • Share a meaningful vision, lead by example, involve developers in decisions, provide ownership and learning opportunities, remove obstacles and recognize contributions.
  • Avoid unclear roles, constant frantic work and repeated unmanaged failure, which undermine confidence and collaboration.
Exam line

Share a vision, model good practice, enable ownership and learning, remove obstacles and recognize contributions.

Source: W2 Ch24 s4,6-7 · Notes: Leadership

Fall 2025 · Question 6 · 3 marks

How does modular design make testing and debugging easier?

What to write

  • Focused modules with explicit interfaces can be tested independently, and a failure can be localized to a smaller area.
  • Information hiding and low coupling limit side effects when a module is corrected.
  • Integration tests remain necessary; splitting into excessive tiny modules can increase complexity.
Exam line

High cohesion, low coupling and explicit interfaces support isolated tests and local fixes. Integration tests still check cooperation.

Source: W4 Ch06 s4-5; W6 Ch09 s8-13; W7 Ch11 s7-8 · Notes: Modularity

Fall 2025 · Question 7 · 3 marks

How does a use case diagram help understand user needs?

What to write

  • It identifies external actors, their goals and the system boundary, showing which users interact with which capabilities.
  • Stakeholders can review the scope and spot missing users/goals.
  • It does not replace the detailed scenarios, alternative flows or NFRs.
Exam line

Use cases expose actors, goals and system scope. Detailed scenarios, alternative flows and NFRs complete the requirements.

Source: W4 Ch07 s8-10; W5 Ch08 s7-13 · Notes: Use case diagrams

Fall 2025 · Question 8 · 3 marks

Why is good communication important in a software team?

What to write

  • It creates a shared understanding of requirements, responsibilities, interfaces and progress.
  • Early questions and recorded decisions reduce inconsistent assumptions, duplicated work, integration defects and delays.
  • Use appropriate formal records together with quick informal collaboration.
Exam line

Good communication builds shared understanding of requirements, roles, interfaces and progress, reducing assumptions, rework and delay.

Source: W2 Ch24 s8-9; W4 Ch06 s7-9 · Notes: Communication

Fall 2025 · Question 9 · 3 marks

Why should we estimate cost and time before starting a project?

What to write

  • Estimates support feasibility decisions, budgets, staffing, scope negotiation and realistic schedules.
  • They provide a baseline for tracking progress and identifying overruns.
  • Because they depend on uncertain information, use comparable historical data, independent checks and revision as knowledge improves.
Exam line

Estimate before commitment to assess feasibility, budget, staffing and schedule, then track against a baseline and revise using evidence.

Source: W2 Ch25 s2-11,21-23 · Notes: Planning

Fall 2025 · Question 10 · 3 marks

How can a team prepare for unexpected technical problems?

What to write

  • Identify important uncertainties, investigate high-risk technology with prototypes/proofs of concept, prepare fallback/contingency actions and allocate appropriate reserves.
  • Monitor progress and new risks, communicate issues promptly and revise plans when evidence changes.
  • Preparation limits impact; it cannot eliminate every unexpected event.
Exam line

Identify risks, test high-risk technology, prepare contingencies and reserves, monitor evidence and revise plans promptly.

Source: W4 Ch06 s3,10-12; W3 Ch04 s15,19; W2 Ch25 s22,25 · Notes: Risk decisions
